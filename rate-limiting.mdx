---
title: "Rate limiting"
description: "Up to 20 requests of burst per API key, refilling at 10 requests per second."
---

Requests are metered **per API key** with a token bucket. The bucket holds at most **20 requests** (the burst) and refills at **10 requests per second**. A key that stays at or below 10 requests per second is never throttled. A burst of up to 20 back-to-back requests is absorbed, after which the key is limited to the refill rate. Test and live keys are metered separately.

## Response headers

Every metered response carries the current state of your bucket:

| Header | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `RateLimit-Limit` | Bucket capacity: the requests available in a full window (`20`) |
| `RateLimit-Remaining` | Requests you can still make right now |
| `RateLimit-Reset` | Seconds until the bucket is back at full capacity |

## When you exceed the limit

The request is rejected with `429 Too Many Requests` **without being processed**: nothing is created, charged or delivered. The response adds a `Retry-After` header with the whole number of seconds to wait, and the body has the usual [error shape](/errors).

```json
{
  "error_code": 429,
  "error_message": "Rate limit exceeded"
}
```

Retry no earlier than `Retry-After`, ideally with exponential backoff and jitter. A `429` is always safe to retry, because the refused request had no effect.

<Note>
  If your integration has a legitimate peak above these values, such as a month-end bulk import, contact us and we will raise the limit for your key.
</Note>
